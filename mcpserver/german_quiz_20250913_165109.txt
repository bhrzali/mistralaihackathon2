TOPIC: Die Vergangenheit im Deutschen (Präteritum und Perfekt)
EXPLANATION: This topic is crucial for beginners to describe completed actions and events in the past, a fundamental aspect of daily communication. It covers both the simple past (Präteritum), often used for narratives and specific verbs like 'sein' and 'haben', and the conversational past (Perfekt), commonly used in spoken German for most actions.

AI_CORRECTION: No correction needed
EXPLANATION: To form the Perfekt tense in German for most verbs, you use a form of "haben" (to have) as the helping verb, followed by the past participle (Partizip II) at the end of the sentence. The verb "machen" (to do/make) uses "haben". Since the subject is "ich", the correct form is "habe".

***
TRANSLATION: Which sentence has the correct word order in the simple past?
AI_ANSWER: Gestern sah ich den Film nicht.

The negation "nicht" usually appears before the object it negates or at the end of the sentence.
